### About & Event Basics (21)

**Q: What is the International AI Builders Congress?**
A: The International AI Builders Congress is one of the largest gatherings of AI builders, product innovators, entrepreneurs, researchers, industry leaders, investors and policymakers from Bangladesh and around the world. It is positioned as a Congress — not a hackathon or classroom competition — focused on building real, deployable AI systems with local relevance and global potential.

**Q: What is the International AI Builders Congress 2026?**
A: The International AI Builders Congress 2026 is a six-month builder journey — not a conference and not a hackathon — designed to transform participants into world-class AI product builders capable of creating globally scalable products, venture-backed startups, intellectual property, jobs, and measurable economic value. It runs from July through December 2026 and culminates on 26 December 2026 at Daffodil International University, Ashulia Campus, Dhaka.

**Q: When and where is the Congress?**
A: Congress Day: Saturday, 26 December 2026, 07:00–19:30 BST. Venue: Daffodil International University, Ashulia Campus, Dhaka, Bangladesh. The wider program launches on 1 July 2026 and runs for six months up to Congress Day.

**Q: Why is it called a Congress and not a hackathon?**
A: A hackathon is a short sprint. The Congress is a six-month builder journey: learn, build, collaborate, mentor, validate and launch. It culminates in a Global Showcase Day where 5,000+ builders gather to present production-ready innovations, compete, and meet investors and employers.

**Q: Who organises the Congress?**
A: The International AI Builders Congress 2026 is organised by CloudCamp with Daffodil International University as the host venue partner, together with a global network of Non-Resident Bangladeshi (NRB) mentors, industry partners and academic collaborators.

**Q: What is the official theme?**
A: Build Products. Create Jobs. Grow Economies. Solve Global Problems. — a global gathering of AI builders, innovators and nation makers, framed as 'From Bangladesh to the World: Building Talent, Building Products, Building Companies, Building Nations.'

**Q: How is this different from other AI events in Bangladesh?**
A: The Congress is outcome-focused rather than presentation-focused. Instead of demo days, it builds a six-month pipeline that produces production-ready AI products, hire-ready talent, investment-ready teams and defensible IP, evaluated by international judges against a rubric used for real venture decisions.

**Q: Will the event be streamed or recorded?**
A: Yes. Keynotes, panels, awards and selected showcase sessions on Congress Day are livestreamed and recorded. Finalist pitches and awards are also captured for post-event distribution to sponsors, investors and media partners.

**Q: Is there a participation fee?**
A: There is no participation fee for the Congress program. Participants are responsible for their own travel, accommodation and food on Congress Day. Some optional workshops and certifications during the six-month journey may carry a small fee, and these are always announced in advance.

**Q: What language is the Congress conducted in?**
A: English is the primary language for all official documents, submissions, judging and international sessions. Bangla is used in selected sessions and mentoring conversations to keep the program accessible to first-time builders across Bangladesh.

**Q: Where can I see the full event schedule?**
A: The Congress Day agenda is published in Section 16 of the Participants Guide and mirrored in the Event Page schedule tab. High-level milestones for the six-month journey are in Section 3 (Official Program Timeline).

**Q: How many people will attend Congress Day?**
A: The Congress is designed for 5,000+ AI builders under one roof, plus mentors, judges, investors, employers, media and government representatives.

**Q: What is the vision of the Congress?**
A: To position Bangladesh not merely as a user of AI, but as a trusted global source of AI-native builders, teams and solutions — creating globally scalable products, venture-backed startups, intellectual property, jobs and measurable economic value.

**Q: What are the two flagship competitions?**
A: 1) Idea to Unicorn — a team-based, company-building competition where teams build a production-ready AI product across the full six months. 2) Zero to Launch — a single-prompt, one-hour individual challenge on Congress Day.

**Q: Can I participate in both competitions?**
A: Yes. Participation in Idea to Unicorn (team-based, six months) and Zero to Launch (individual, one hour on Congress Day) is independent. Many participants do both.

**Q: What does the six-month builder journey look like?**
A: July — onboarding and skill baseline. August — team formation and idea validation. September — prototype build. October — production hardening and testing with real users. November — preliminary submission and evaluation. December — final build, Congress Day showcase, judging and awards.

**Q: What is the strategic goal of the Congress?**
A: To connect Bangladesh's young talent with global NRB expertise so participants build real AI systems — not just learn about them — and become major contributors to the global digital workforce and to sovereign, trustworthy AI.

**Q: What is the NRB Builder Framework?**
A: A global collaboration model that pairs Bangladeshi teams with Non-Resident Bangladeshi (NRB) mentors and industry practitioners so local builders get direct exposure to global standards, feedback loops, hiring networks and investor pipelines.

**Q: How do I stay updated about the Congress?**
A: Log in to your CloudCamp account and follow the event page for the International AI Builders Congress. Announcements are delivered inside the platform, and important updates are also sent by email to registered participants.

**Q: Is the Congress open to international participants?**
A: Yes. International participants — especially NRB professionals and teams from partner universities abroad — are welcome as builders, mentors, judges, sponsors and observers. The Congress is deliberately international in scope.

**Q: Who should I contact for general questions?**
A: Use the floating help button on the event page to open the Participants Guide chat. For direct support, contact the organisers via the Contact section on the event page or your assigned mentor.

### Registration & Eligibility (24)

**Q: Who can register for the Congress?**
A: Students, developers, designers, product builders, founders, researchers, industry professionals and multidisciplinary teams are welcome. NRB professionals and international collaborators may join as team members or contributors.

**Q: Is there an age or institution requirement?**
A: There is no upper age limit. Participants must be 16 or older by 26 December 2026. You do not need to be enrolled at a specific university — anyone meeting the eligibility criteria can apply.

**Q: When does registration open and close?**
A: Registration is open from 12 June 2026 and closes on 30 June 2026, 23:59 Bangladesh time. The Preliminary Submission window then runs from 30 June to 12 August 2026.

**Q: Do I need to register a team or can I sign up alone?**
A: You can register as an individual first. After registering you can either create a team and invite others, join an existing team using its invite code, or use the Team Discovery page to find a team.

**Q: Can international participants and NRB professionals join?**
A: Yes. Teams may include NRB professionals or international experts as members or contributors — for example in architecture, product strategy, compliance, market validation or deployment standards.

**Q: Do I need to complete my profile fully?**
A: Yes. Your participant profile must be 100 percent complete before you can create a team, join a team, or submit. This includes identity, skills, links and consent.

**Q: I forgot my password. How do I reset it?**
A: Use the Forgot password link on the sign-in page. A reset link will be emailed to you. After resetting, you will be signed out and asked to sign in again with the new password.

**Q: Can one person register on behalf of the whole team?**
A: No. Every team member must create their own account, complete their own profile, and accept the consent terms individually. The team leader then invites them using the team invite code or link.

**Q: Will I get a confirmation after registration?**
A: Yes. You will see a registration ID on your dashboard in the format CC-YYYY-EVENTCODE-SEQUENCE, and an email confirmation will be sent to the address you registered with.

**Q: Who is eligible to participate?**
A: Anyone 16 years or older can participate. Students, professionals, researchers, freelancers, entrepreneurs and NRB professionals are all welcome. Some university-track sub-competitions are limited to enrolled students; those restrictions are noted where they apply.

**Q: Is there an age limit?**
A: The minimum age is 16 on Congress Day. Participants under 18 must have written consent from a parent or legal guardian, submitted during registration.

**Q: How do I register?**
A: Open the event page for the International AI Builders Congress 2026, click Register, sign in or create a CloudCamp account, complete your participant profile, accept the Code of Conduct and IP terms, and confirm your registration.

**Q: Is there a registration deadline?**
A: Registration for Idea to Unicorn closes before the Preliminary Submission window opens. Registration for Zero to Launch remains open until Congress Day capacity is reached. Exact dates are shown on the event page.

**Q: Do I need to be from Bangladesh to join?**
A: No. The Congress is international. Participants, mentors, judges and observers from any country are welcome. The venue and majority of participants will be in Bangladesh, but remote-friendly participation options are provided during the six-month journey.

**Q: Can university students join as a team?**
A: Yes. University teams are strongly encouraged. Many partner universities are running internal cohorts and sending their top teams to the Congress.

**Q: Do I need prior AI experience to join?**
A: No. The six-month journey includes training modules, mentor sessions and reference architectures designed for first-time AI builders as well as experienced practitioners.

**Q: Can working professionals participate?**
A: Yes. Working professionals can participate on evenings and weekends. Some employers are running internal builder cohorts and sending teams to the Congress.

**Q: What information do I need to register?**
A: Full legal name, email, phone, city and country, institution or company, role (student or professional), skills, background summary, and links to LinkedIn/GitHub/portfolio if you have them.

**Q: Do I need to upload documents at registration?**
A: No documents are required at registration. Participants under 18 must upload a signed parental consent form before they can join a team or submit.

**Q: Can I edit my profile after registering?**
A: Yes. You can edit your participant profile at any time from your dashboard. Some fields (full legal name for certificates) require a small verification step to change.

**Q: Can I withdraw after registering?**
A: Yes. You can withdraw at any time before the final submission deadline. If you are a team leader, you must first transfer leadership or the team will be disbanded.

**Q: Is there a waitlist if registration is full?**
A: For Zero to Launch, once the Congress Day venue capacity is reached, additional applicants are moved to a waitlist and offered spots as cancellations open up. Idea to Unicorn is not capacity-limited during the six-month journey.

**Q: Are refunds available if I paid for an optional workshop?**
A: Refunds for optional paid workshops follow the refund policy shown at checkout for that workshop — typically full refund up to 7 days before the workshop and 50% within 7 days.

**Q: How do I know my registration is confirmed?**
A: You will see a 'Registered' badge on the event page, your dashboard will show the event under 'My Events', and you will receive a confirmation email with your registration identifier in the format CC-2026-<event code>-<sequence>.

### Teams & Collaboration (29)

**Q: How many members can a team have?**
A: A team must have between 3 and 5 members. Solo submissions are not allowed.

**Q: Can I be in more than one team?**
A: No. Each participant can be on only one team for the Congress to keep judging and prize allocation clean.

**Q: How do I invite teammates?**
A: From your team page, copy the invite code or invite link and share it with your teammates. They register, accept consent, then paste the code or open the link to join.

**Q: What if I don't have a team yet?**
A: Open the Team Discovery page after registering. You can list yourself as looking for a team, browse open teams with positions to fill, and send a join request or accept an invite.

**Q: Can I leave my team or transfer leadership?**
A: Yes, before the relevant deadline. The team leader can transfer leadership to another member, and any member can leave the team. After a submission is finalised, member changes are restricted.

**Q: Can my team include people from different universities or companies?**
A: Yes. Cross-institution and cross-organisation teams are encouraged. The team leader is the single point of contact for that team.

**Q: Do teams need to upload a team logo?**
A: Yes. A team logo is required for the team profile and is used on certificates, the showcase page and the public team card.

**Q: How do we change our team name or domain after creating?**
A: The team leader can edit the team name, tagline and logo from the team page until the Preliminary Submission deadline. Changing domain after submission is restricted and requires Admin approval.

**Q: What if a teammate becomes inactive?**
A: Before the submission deadline, the team leader can remove the inactive member and invite a replacement. After the deadline, contact event support with a written explanation.

**Q: What is the team size for Idea to Unicorn?**
A: Teams must have a minimum of 3 and a maximum of 6 members. The recommended composition is 1 product lead, 2–3 builders (engineering and AI), 1 domain expert, and 1 growth or business lead.

**Q: Is Zero to Launch individual or team-based?**
A: Zero to Launch is strictly individual. One person, one prompt, one hour. Team collaboration or shared prompting is not allowed during the challenge.

**Q: How do I create a team?**
A: From your dashboard, open the Teams section for the Congress event, click Create Team, choose a name, tagline and optional logo. You become the team leader automatically.

**Q: How do I invite people to my team?**
A: As team leader, open your team page and use the join code or the tokenised invite link. You can also send email invites to registered participants directly from the team page.

**Q: Can I join more than one team?**
A: No. Each participant can belong to only one Idea to Unicorn team per event. Trying to join a second team requires leaving your current team first.

**Q: Can I change teams after joining?**
A: Yes, before the team-lock deadline. You can leave your team from the team page. If you are the team leader, you must transfer leadership first. After the team-lock deadline, team changes require organiser approval.

**Q: When is the team-lock deadline?**
A: Teams lock at the start of the Preliminary Submission window. After lock, no member can join, leave or be removed without organiser approval. The exact date is shown on the event page timeline.

**Q: Can I have fewer than 3 members?**
A: No. Teams with fewer than 3 members at team-lock are automatically marked incomplete and are ineligible for judging in Idea to Unicorn. Solo builders should join Zero to Launch instead.

**Q: Can I have more than 6 members?**
A: No. The system enforces the maximum team size of 6. If you have more contributors, list them as advisors or mentors rather than official team members.

**Q: What is a team leader responsible for?**
A: The team leader manages membership, submits and locks the submission, communicates with mentors and organisers, ensures the Code of Conduct is followed, and represents the team in judging conversations.

**Q: Can I transfer team leadership?**
A: Yes. From the team page, open the member list and choose 'Transfer leadership'. The new leader must already be a member of the team.

**Q: Can I remove a team member?**
A: Yes, before team lock the team leader can remove any member. After team lock, removals require organiser approval and are logged in the audit trail with a reason.

**Q: Can I disband my team?**
A: Yes, before team lock. As team leader, you can dissolve the team from the team page. After team lock, disbanding requires organiser approval.

**Q: How do we collaborate remotely as a team?**
A: The Congress does not host code or files. Teams collaborate on their preferred tools — GitHub for code, Figma for design, Google Drive for documents, Discord/Slack for communication — and link those from the submission.

**Q: Can international members be on our team?**
A: Yes. Cross-border teams are encouraged, especially teams that combine Bangladeshi builders with NRB or international members. All team members must register individually.

**Q: What is the recommended team composition?**
A: 1 product lead, 2–3 builders (engineering and AI), 1 domain expert (relevant to your chosen domain), and 1 growth or business lead. Teams that skip the domain or business roles consistently score lower.

**Q: Can mentors be listed as team members?**
A: No. Mentors are separate from official team members. Mentors are recognised on the submission and in the mentor rating flow but do not count against the 3–6 member limit and cannot receive competition awards.

**Q: How does the invite link work?**
A: The invite link is a tokenised URL that lets any registered participant open your team page and request to join. Rate limits are applied per IP and per user to prevent abuse.

**Q: How does the join code work?**
A: The join code is a 6–8 character non-guessable code. Registered participants can enter it from the Teams tab to request to join your team. The leader may need to approve the join depending on team settings.

**Q: Where do we discuss with our mentor?**
A: Each team gets a mentor thread in the platform once a mentor is assigned. Use that thread so mentor advice, feedback and ratings stay tied to the team record and audit trail.

### Judging & Awards (30)

**Q: How will submissions be judged?**
A: Each submission is scored by a minimum of five independent judges using a weighted rubric: Innovation (20%), Technical Execution (20%), Business Model + Global Readiness (20%), Real-World Impact + Ethical AI Compliance (20%), Scalability + NRB Collaboration (10%), and Presentation (10%). Innovation, Technical Execution, Business Model and Real-World Impact are scored 0–20, while Scalability and Presentation are scored 0–10.

**Q: Who are the judges?**
A: Judges are senior industry professionals, founders, researchers, NRB experts and international collaborators with proven domain experience. All judges declare conflicts of interest before scoring and are masked from teams during evaluation.

**Q: How is the final score calculated?**
A: Each judge produces a weighted total normalized to 0–100 using the rubric weights. The final score is the average of judge totals across the minimum required judges (after excluding any conflict-of-interest judges). When 4 or more judges score, a trimmed mean is used — the highest and lowest are dropped before averaging — and scores are rounded to 2 decimals.

**Q: What happens if judges disagree a lot?**
A: If the gap between the highest and lowest judge totals is 25 or more points, the submission is flagged High Variance and an additional judge is assigned by the Admin team to re-balance the score.

**Q: How are ties broken?**
A: In order: higher average Innovation sub-score, then higher Technical Execution sub-score, then higher Real-World Impact sub-score, then earlier submission timestamp, and finally Admin manual resolution with an audit note.

**Q: What are the prizes?**
A: Each domain has its own prize structure. As an example, the E-Commerce domain awards 50,000 BDT to the Champion and 30,000 BDT to the Runner-Up. See the Awards section on this page for the full list across all ten domains.

**Q: Will there be certificates?**
A: Yes. Every participant who completes a valid submission receives a Participation certificate. Shortlisted, finalist and winning teams receive specific certificates issued through the platform's certificates module.

**Q: Will leaderboards be public?**
A: Public rankings will be released after the Final Program on 26 December 2026. During judging, scores are hidden from participants to keep the process fair.

**Q: What is the disqualification policy?**
A: Plagiarism, undisclosed use of others' proprietary work, broken or fake demo links, harassment, or a violation of the Code of Conduct can result in disqualification with an audit note. Teams may be given an opportunity to respond before a final decision.

**Q: How will winners be announced and paid?**
A: Winners will be announced on stage during the Final Program on 26 December 2026 and on the platform within 24 hours. Prize money is paid via bank or mobile financial services after winner KYC is completed.

**Q: What is the judging rubric?**
A: The default rubric is: Impact (25%), Technical Implementation (25%), AI & Data Use (20%), Innovation & Differentiation (15%), Execution & Presentation (15%). Each criterion is scored 0–10 in 0.5 increments.

**Q: How is my final score calculated?**
A: For each judge: judge_total = 10 × Σ (criterion_score × criterion_weight/100). Across judges: average the top N judge totals (default N = 3, or a trimmed mean when variance is high). Displayed to two decimals; ranked on full precision.

**Q: How many judges score each submission?**
A: A minimum of 3 judges per submission. When variance is high, a fourth judge may be added and a trimmed mean is used instead of a simple average.

**Q: Who selects the judges?**
A: Judges are recruited by the organisers from the international NRB network, partner companies, investors, academic experts and past winners. Every judge signs a Conflict of Interest declaration before scoring.

**Q: What is a Conflict of Interest declaration?**
A: Before scoring, each judge confirms they are not affiliated with, invested in, employed by, or teaching any team member of the submissions in their queue. COI submissions are excluded from that judge's queue.

**Q: Can I see who judged my submission?**
A: No. Judge identities are masked in participant-facing views to protect independence. Aggregated feedback is shared with the team after results are published.

**Q: Will I get feedback on my submission?**
A: Yes. After results are published, each team receives per-criterion averages and combined qualitative feedback from all judges, with individual identities masked.

**Q: What are the score bands?**
A: 0–39 Emerging, 40–59 Promising, 60–74 Strong, 75–89 Excellent, 90–100 World-Class. Only Strong and above are typically eligible for named awards.

**Q: Are there separate rankings per domain?**
A: Yes. Rankings are produced per event, per phase, and per domain by default, with optional per-challenge sub-rankings. Awards are given per domain plus overall Congress awards.

**Q: How does the Impact criterion work?**
A: Impact (25%) rewards how many people the product can help, how measurable the outcome is, and how well the team quantified the impact — pilot users, adoption, cost saved, lives improved, etc.

**Q: How does Technical Implementation work?**
A: Technical (25%) rewards code quality, system architecture, security, reliability under load, and the ability to actually run the product end-to-end without heavy hand-holding.

**Q: How does AI & Data Use work?**
A: AI & Data (20%) rewards responsible, well-chosen model selection, data quality and licensing, evaluation methodology, guardrails, and the sophistication of AI-native features (agents, memory, retrieval, multimodality).

**Q: How does Innovation & Differentiation work?**
A: Innovation (15%) rewards originality of approach, defensible technical or product moat, and how clearly the team can articulate why nobody else has done it this way.

**Q: How does Execution & Presentation work?**
A: Execution (15%) rewards the polish of the demo, the clarity of the submission, the team's ability to tell a compelling story, and evidence of real users or design partners.

**Q: Can I include screenshots or slides in the submission?**
A: Yes. You can attach a slide deck via a link (Google Slides, Figma slides, PDF on Drive). The deck must be accessible under the same PASS/RESTRICTED rules as any other link.

**Q: Can we upload files directly to the platform?**
A: No. The platform does not host files or code — everything is submitted as a link to your preferred hosting (GitHub, Drive, Figma, YouTube, etc.). This keeps your IP under your control.

**Q: How do we handle sensitive personal data in the demo?**
A: Never use real personal data in the public demo. Use synthetic or anonymised data. Disclose the data source and anonymisation approach in the Data & AI provenance section.

**Q: How does judging use our submission?**
A: Assigned judges review the full submission (Judge+Admin view), score each rubric criterion (0–10), leave comments, and lock their score. Aggregation runs automatically to produce a ranked leaderboard per phase, domain and (optionally) challenge.

**Q: Will judges contact us for clarification?**
A: Usually no — judging is based on the submission as it stands. In rare cases a judge lead may request a clarification via the platform. Never share contact details or attempt to reach a judge outside the platform (grounds for disqualification).

**Q: What happens after the Final Submission deadline?**
A: Server-side lock triggers automatically. Judges are assigned within 24 hours, the judging window runs 25–26 December, aggregation and tie-breaking finish on the morning of Congress Day, and results are announced during the awards session on Congress Day evening.

**Q: Can we improve our product after submission?**
A: You can keep working on the product itself, but only what is captured in the Locked submission is judged. Judges evaluate the version at deadline, not any post-deadline improvements.

**Q: How are ties broken in the leaderboard?**
A: In this order: higher average Impact sub-score, then higher average Technical sub-score, then higher average AI & Data sub-score, then earlier submission timestamp, then admin manual resolution (with a written audit note).

**Q: What is the 'variance flag' on scores?**
A: If the highest and lowest judge totals differ by 25+ points and at least three judges scored, the submission is flagged 'High Variance'. An additional judge may be assigned, and with 4+ judges a trimmed mean (drop highest and lowest, average the rest) is used.

**Q: Can we appeal a judging outcome?**
A: You can raise a formal review request within 48 hours of results being published. Reviews are handled by the appeals panel and are limited to procedural questions (missing judges, calculation errors, rubric misapplication) — not disagreement with subjective scores.

**Q: Can we withdraw our submission after locking?**
A: Yes. The team leader can request withdrawal at any time. Withdrawn submissions are removed from judging and ranking, and this is recorded in the audit trail.

**Q: How do we export our submission for our records?**
A: From the submission page, use 'Export as PDF' to download a redacted PDF of your submission. Proprietary fields are included in the team leader's copy and redacted from public exports.

**Q: What is the NRB Collaboration Award?**

**Q: What is the People's Choice Award?**

**Q: How are prize funds distributed to a team?**
A: Cash prizes are wired to the team leader's designated bank account within 30 days of Congress Day, subject to tax documentation. Teams are responsible for internal distribution among members.

### Policies & Consent (15)

**Q: What policies do I agree to at registration?**
A: The Code of Conduct & Participation Agreement (which covers professional conduct, original work, responsible AI, data protection, IP, NRB collaboration integrity, fair competition, media consent, security and disqualification) and the platform's Terms of Service and Privacy Policy.

**Q: Do I keep the IP of what I build?**
A: Yes. Teams retain full ownership of the code, models, prompts and product they build. The Congress and its sponsors get a limited, non-exclusive right to showcase, reference and publicise the submission for promotional purposes.

**Q: Do sponsors get access to my code?**
A: No. Sponsors do not receive team code, prompts, or proprietary submission fields. Sponsors see the public and showcase-level fields, and may request formal introductions through the platform.

**Q: What is 'Original Work' under the Code of Conduct?**
A: Original Work means the design, code, prompts and product concept are yours or your team's, or are used under a valid open-source/commercial licence with proper attribution. Copying another team's work is grounds for disqualification.

**Q: Am I allowed to use AI to help me write code?**
A: Yes. AI-assisted coding is expected and encouraged. You must disclose the tools, IDEs and coding assistants used in the tooling section, and prompts used for major features in the Prompt Library.

**Q: Am I allowed to use open-source models and libraries?**
A: Yes, provided you respect their licences and attribute them in your Data & AI provenance section. Some licences (e.g. non-commercial) may limit how you can use the model in a commercial product.

**Q: How is my personal data used?**
A: Personal data is used to operate the Congress (registration, judging, comms) and to send you information about the event. It is not sold. Full details are in the Privacy Policy on the event page.

**Q: Can I opt out of media consent?**
A: Yes. Media consent is optional and separate from the Code of Conduct. If you opt out, you will not appear in photos, videos or press content. Notify the organisers in advance so you can be seated in a no-camera zone during Congress Day.

**Q: What is 'Responsible AI' under the policy?**
A: Avoiding unlawful or harmful applications, preventing bias, discrimination or unsafe model outputs, implementing transparency in AI reasoning where possible, and avoiding systems that promote misinformation, fraud, harm or manipulation.

**Q: What counts as prohibited security activity?**
A: Introducing malware, exploiting vulnerabilities, interfering with event infrastructure, accessing unauthorised systems or misusing the event platform. Any of these is grounds for immediate disqualification and possible legal action.

**Q: What is NRB collaboration integrity?**
A: Cross-border collaboration must be substantive — real work with an NRB mentor or contributor. Listing an NRB name without meaningful involvement to qualify for the NRB Collaboration Award is a policy violation.

**Q: What are the grounds for disqualification?**
A: Plagiarism, faked demos, ghost-authorship by non-team members, harassment, cheating, misrepresentation, sharing another team's proprietary fields, breaking the Zero to Launch 'one prompt' rule, or security violations.

**Q: Is there an appeals process for disqualification?**
A: Yes. Teams may submit a written appeal within 48 hours of the disqualification notice. Appeals are reviewed by an independent panel and the decision is final.

**Q: Do policies apply after the Congress ends?**
A: The IP, confidentiality, media consent and defamation clauses survive the end of the Congress. Everything else applies while you are a registered participant.

**Q: Where can I read the full Code of Conduct?**
A: Section 20 of the Participants Guide contains the full Code of Conduct & Participation Agreement. It is also linked from the event page footer and shown in-line during registration for acceptance.
